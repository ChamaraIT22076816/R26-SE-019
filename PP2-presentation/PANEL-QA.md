# Panel Q&A — Learn module

24 questions the panel is likely to ask, with answers grounded in the code.
Every claim here was verified against the repository on 31 Aug 2026, not
recalled. Where the honest answer is "we haven't done that", it says so — those
are marked **⚠ weak spot** with the best available framing.

Companion documents: [DEFENCE.md](DEFENCE.md) for metric/dataset/methodology
depth, [EVIDENCE.md](EVIDENCE.md) for reproduction.

---

## 1. What is this Learn component?

> "It's a practice tutor for **hearing people learning Sri Lankan Sign
> Language**. The other three components translate between Deaf and hearing
> users. Mine reduces the need for translation in the first place, by teaching
> the hearing side to sign.
>
> You pick a sign, watch a reference performed by a real signer, perform it to
> your webcam, and get a score out of 100 plus specific corrective feedback —
> which finger was wrong, whether your movement was off, whether you were too
> fast or too slow. It tracks what you're weak at and picks what you practise
> next."

**Do not call it a recogniser.** It grades a sign you were *asked* to perform.
That distinction is §15 and §18.

## 2. How did you build this?

> "Seven weeks, in the order the risk demanded. Weeks 1–2: the React app,
> in-browser hand tracking, a tool to convert dataset video into reference
> landmark sequences, and DTW scoring. Weeks 3–4: the feedback overlay and
> corrective hints, plus latency instrumentation. Week 5: the learner model and
> progress dashboard. Week 6: the scenario mode and the vocabulary expansion —
> 490 signs, well past our 20–30 target. Week 7: calibration against real data,
> the UI pass, and deployment.
>
> The evaluation was built alongside, not bolted on: the reports in the repo are
> generated by the test suite, so a number and the code that produced it can't
> disagree."

Supporting: **129 automated tests**, ~9,500 lines of TypeScript, ~2,350 of it
tests. 59 commits.

## 3. How does this work?

Five stages. Say them in order:

> "One — the webcam frame goes to **MediaPipe HandLandmarker**, which returns 21
> 3-D landmarks per hand. That's the only neural network in my component and it
> runs in the browser.
>
> Two — **normalisation**. Landmarks are made wrist-relative and scaled by hand
> size, so it doesn't matter where you stand or how far you are from the camera.
> That splits into a *handshape* block and a *trajectory* block — the wrist path.
>
> Three — **Dynamic Time Warping** aligns your sequence against the reference
> recording. DTW absorbs speed differences, so signing slowly doesn't penalise
> you.
>
> Four — the alignment gives a distance, which maps to **0–100** on a scale
> fitted to measured data, and per-joint deviations become the corrective text.
>
> Five — the attempt is logged locally, which updates your mastery and what the
> app suggests next."

Two details worth volunteering because they show care:

- **Every attempt is scored twice — as performed and mirrored** — and the better
  result wins. Sign languages let you choose your dominant hand, so a
  left-dominant learner performing a right-handed reference is signing
  *correctly* and must not be marked as missing a hand.
- **The capture window is `reference duration + 1500 ms`**, minimum 2500 ms, so a
  slower learner still fits inside it.

## 4. What are the proven research gaps?

Four, in descending strength:

1. **SSL is under-served.** Sign-language AI overwhelmingly targets ASL. Sri
   Lankan Sign Language has very little tooling, and none of it teaches.
2. **Existing sign-language systems recognise or translate; they do not tutor.**
   A classifier returns a label and a confidence. Neither can tell a learner
   *which finger was wrong*. That gap is why my component uses an alignment
   method rather than a classifier — §15.
3. **There is no corpus of SSL *learner* attempts.** Everything public is fluent
   signers performing correctly. That is exactly the data a learning system
   needs and the reason our ≥90%-vs-expert target is still unmeasured.
4. **Two-way systems are rare.** Most work solves one direction. The project as a
   whole closes the loop; my component removes translation from the loop
   entirely for signs the hearing user has learned.

## 5. Tech stack — and what each thing does

| Layer | Choice | Its job |
|---|---|---|
| UI | **React 19 + TypeScript, Vite 8** | The whole application; TS because the scoring code is maths and types catch shape errors early |
| Hand tracking | **MediaPipe Tasks Vision** (`HandLandmarker`) | 21 landmarks per hand per frame, in-browser, GPU via WebGL with CPU fallback |
| Scoring | **Own DTW implementation** (`src/scoring/`) | Alignment, distance, per-joint deviation, corrective hints |
| Storage | **IndexedDB** (`ssl-learn`, v3, 3 stores) | Attempts, latency samples, locally-recorded references — all on the learner's device |
| Reference pipeline | **Python + OpenCV + MediaPipe** (`tools/convert_references.py`) | Offline: dataset video → landmark JSON. Never runs in production |
| Tests | **Vitest** | 129 tests; also the generator for the three evaluation reports |
| Charts | **Matplotlib** | The nine evaluation figures |
| Motion / icons | GSAP, Lenis, lucide-react | Hero animation, smooth scroll, icons |
| Hosting | **Vercel**, static | No backend at all |

**There is no server.** No database, no API, no auth service. That is a design
decision, not an omission — §10 and §22.

## 6. Why that stack?

> "Four reasons, in order of weight.
>
> **Web, because a demo must not need an install.** We're testing with
> participants and presenting to panels. A desktop Python app — which is what our
> PP1 prototype was — costs you every user who won't install something. It also
> matches my teammate's Next.js stack, which makes integration a routing problem
> instead of a rewrite.
>
> **MediaPipe in the browser, because it removes the server.** Landmark inference
> on-device means no video upload, which makes the privacy claim structural
> rather than a promise, and makes ethics approval straightforward. It also
> removes latency and hosting cost.
>
> **DTW, because the output has to be explainable.** A learner needs to know
> *what* was wrong, not just that something was. DTW gives an alignment path, so
> deviation is attributable to a joint at a moment. And it needs no training
> data per sign — one reference recording is enough, which matters because SSL
> learner data doesn't exist.
>
> **IndexedDB rather than a backend**, so a participant's practice data never
> leaves their machine."

## 8. Are you planning to create your own dataset?

> "Yes, and it's the highest-value thing left. Two datasets, both recorded with a
> teacher at the School for the Deaf, Ratmalana:
>
> **Signer-validated reference video.** Right now our references are landmark
> skeletons derived from public corpora. A skeleton can't convey palm
> orientation or fine handshape detail, and nothing in our library has been
> reviewed by a Deaf signer for this project. Our own recordings would be the
> first references we could present as authoritative rather than provisional —
> and we'd own the licence, which §13 depends on.
>
> **Learner attempts graded by a teacher.** This is the one that unlocks the
> ≥90%-against-expert target. Every public corpus is fluent signers performing
> correctly; nobody has a corpus of *learners getting it wrong* with expert
> grades attached. That's the dataset our component actually needs, and
> collecting it is a genuine research contribution in itself."

## 9. How is your work ethical?

Six points; lead with the strongest.

1. **No video ever leaves the device**, and that's structural — there is no code
   path that could send it (§22). Consent is simple because there's nothing to
   consent to sharing.
2. **No personal data.** The pilot export carries a **participant code**, not a
   name. Attempts store gloss, score, worst fingers and a timestamp — never
   frames.
3. **Nothing about SSL is invented.** No vocabulary, gloss, handshape or sentence
   order was ever authored by us or generated by AI. Every sign comes from a
   published corpus performed by real signers. Anything needing a certified
   signer is deferred to the School for the Deaf. A plausible-sounding invented
   sign would be worse than a missing one.
4. **Limitations are disclosed in the product, not just the report.** The
   non-manual-marker deviation is rendered in the app's own scenario summary.
   The Restaurant scenario file carries a `_draftNote` saying its conversational
   order is pending validation by an SSL teacher.
5. **Deliberately no dark patterns** — §20.
6. **Licences are respected and enforced by a test** — §7.

**⚠ Weak spot, name it before they do:** *no Deaf person has used or validated
this yet.* We are a hearing team building from public corpora. The partnership
exists; the validation is the next phase.

## 10. How is your work secure?

> "The honest answer is that the attack surface is very small because there
> isn't a backend. No server, no database, no API, no accounts, no credentials,
> no cookies, no session tokens. Nothing to breach, and nothing of the user's
> held anywhere but their own browser.
>
> The deployment is static files on Vercel over HTTPS. Every asset — including
> the 11 MB MediaPipe runtime and the 7.8 MB model — is served from our own
> origin rather than a CDN, so there's no third-party script executing in the
> page. I verified that: the only absolute URLs in the built bundle are W3C XML
> namespaces and two documentation links. No endpoint, no analytics, no
> tracker."

**⚠ Be honest if pushed on `?mode=author`.** The author/researcher tabs are
gated by a URL parameter plus a `localStorage` flag. **That is a UI mode, not a
security boundary** — it's discoverable. It is acceptable *only* because it
exposes nothing sensitive: it reveals reference-recording tools that operate on
the user's own device and their own data. If this ever gains a backend, that
becomes a real authorisation problem and would need real auth.

## 11. How good is your product in performance?

| Dimension | Figure |
|---|---|
| Scoring stage | median **1.8 ms**, p95 12.6 ms — against a 300 ms budget |
| Discrimination | **ROC AUC 0.744**; 74.6% vs a **65.4% majority baseline** |
| Operational separation | **28.7-point** score gap (correct 56.7 vs wrong 28.0) |
| Correctness | **129 automated tests** covering scoring invariances |
| Vocabulary | 490 glosses, 501 references |
| First load | MediaPipe (~135 KB JS + 11 MB WASM + 7.8 MB model) is **lazy-loaded** — a learner who never starts the camera never pays for it |

**Never say "1.8 ms feedback latency".** It's the *scoring stage*: DTW between
the finished attempt and the reference. It excludes landmark inference, React's
commit and the browser's paint, and it ran in Node, not a participant's browser.
The end-to-end path is instrumented and reports live in the Progress tab; it
needs ~20 real attempts before its p95 means anything.

## 12. Why should customers use this?

> "Four reasons.
>
> **It teaches Sri Lankan Sign Language.** Almost nothing does. If you want to
> learn SSL today your options are a class or a person.
>
> **It corrects you specifically.** A video course can't see you. This tells you
> which finger is wrong and whether your movement was off — that's the
> difference between practice and just watching.
>
> **Nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.** It's a web page. Open it and
> start.
>
> **Your video never leaves your machine.** For a camera pointed at your face and
> hands in your home, that matters, and we can prove it rather than promise it."

## 13. How can you commercialize this?

**Lead with the blocker. It's the mature answer and they may already know.**

> "There's a licence blocker we have to solve first, and I'd rather name it. Most
> of our reference data comes from a corpus licensed **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** —
> non-commercial and share-alike. We cannot sell a product built on it. That's
> precisely why recording our own dataset with the School for the Deaf isn't
> just a quality improvement, it's the commercial unlock: references we own,
> licensed how we choose.
>
> With that solved, the realistic routes are:
>
> **Institutional licensing** — schools, universities and hospitals training
> staff to communicate with Deaf clients. That's a per-seat B2B sale to an
> organisation with a budget and a compliance reason to buy.
>
> **Public-sector and accessibility mandates** — government service desks,
> transport, healthcare front-line staff.
>
> **Partnership with Deaf schools and associations**, where a revenue share funds
> the dataset work and the community that validated it benefits from it. Given
> where the data comes from, I think that's the ethically correct model, not
> just a nice one.
>
> **Free for individual learners**, because the cost to serve is nearly zero —
> it's static hosting with no inference cost. Adoption there is what makes the
> institutional sale credible."

## 14. Why only hands? Why not face and body?

> "Because we track hand landmarks only, and reporting a number for something we
> don't capture would be fabricating it.
>
> Non-manual markers — facial expression, head tilt, body movement — are
> linguistically meaningful in SSL, and our proposal allocated them 10% of a
> scenario turn's score. We use MediaPipe **HandLandmarker**: 21 points per hand,
> nothing else. So we reallocated that 10% to accuracy, giving 50/30/20, and we
> declared the deviation in three places — the source, the README, and the app's
> own scenario summary, so a user meets it without reading our code.
>
> Adding it is a solved engineering problem: MediaPipe also has FaceLandmarker
> and PoseLandmarker. It's future work rather than a limitation of the approach —
> and it would need a second calibration, because we'd be scoring a signal our
> current corpus was never validated for."

There's also a related design rule worth quoting: **unmeasurable ≠ zero.** If a
rubric component has no data it reports *n/a* and its weight is redistributed.
Scoring it zero would penalise the learner for a gap in our data.

## 15. How does the scoring work?

> "Per frame, each hand becomes two feature blocks: **handshape** — 21 landmarks
> made wrist-relative and scaled by hand size — and **trajectory**, the wrist
> path, centred and scaled. There's aspect-ratio correction so a 16:9 frame
> doesn't distort distances.
>
> DTW aligns the two sequences and gives an average per-frame distance in
> hand-size units. That distance maps linearly to 0–100 between two anchors:
> **0.22 scores 100, 0.77 scores 0**. Those aren't chosen — they're the 10th and
> 90th percentile of the distance between two correct renditions, measured over
> 557 takes. So the best tenth of correct attempts score 100 and the worst tenth
> score 0.
>
> The same alignment gives per-landmark deviations, which map to fingers. A
> finger only earns a hint if its average deviation exceeds 0.1 hand-size units,
> otherwise a near-perfect attempt gets nagged about fingers that are fine.
>
> Two hands are handled separately and frame-weighted. If the reference uses a
> hand you never showed, that hand scores 0 — but only after we've also tried
> the mirrored version, in case you're left-dominant."

**The killer follow-up to volunteer:** *the shipped system never applies a
threshold.* The 74.6% separation figure is an evaluation device for
characterising the distance metric. What a learner gets is the continuous score.

**Weights:** handshape 0.8, trajectory 0.2 — fitted by grid search, and
deliberately **not** the maximum. Full reasoning in [DEFENCE.md](DEFENCE.md) §3.2;
it's the single best "tell us about a design decision" answer available.

## 16. How does the suggested sign / personalisation work?

> "Every sign gets a **practice need** score between 0 and 1, and the highest
> wins.
>
> A sign you've never attempted scores **1.0** — breadth before depth, introduce
> new vocabulary first.
>
> For a sign you have attempted, need is **75% weakness plus 25% staleness**.
> Weakness is one minus your mastery. Mastery is a recency-weighted average
> where each new attempt moves the estimate halfway — so improving over time
> beats declining, which a plain mean wouldn't capture. Staleness ramps to full
> over five days, so a sign you've mastered resurfaces for revision instead of
> disappearing.
>
> Mastery bands are New, Learning, Improving, Mastered — and Mastered needs at
> least three attempts, so one lucky score can't earn it.
>
> A practice session is that same ranking generalised from one sign to five."

**It is a heuristic, and say so.** Full BKT plus reinforcement-learning
curriculum is the final phase (Sep–Oct). It was deliberately kept simple and
explainable for PP2, and the attempt log it reads from doesn't change when the
model is swapped — so that upgrade is contained.

## 17. Why is the suggested sign ADINAWA rather than something else?

**Answer this one plainly. The honest answer is better than a clever one.**

> "Because you're a brand-new learner, so there's no signal to personalise on.
> Every sign has never been attempted, so every sign scores the maximum need of
> 1.0 — it's a 490-way tie. Something has to break it, and the tie-break is
> alphabetical.
>
> There's one refinement: among signs of equal need it prefers a **word** over a
> numeral or a fingerspelled letter, because our corpus's alphabetically earliest
> labels are numerals — you'd otherwise open on '1', '100', '1000'. ADINAWA is
> the alphabetically first non-numeric gloss. That preference only ever breaks
> near-ties; it can never promote a sign you need less.
>
> **Make one attempt and it stops being alphabetical** — from then on it's ranked
> by your weakness and how long since you practised."

If asked whether that's good pedagogy: no, and it's a known limit. A
curriculum-ordered cold start — frequency, difficulty, or a teacher's ordering —
is exactly what the BKT/RL work in the final phase addresses. The reason it
isn't in yet is that ordering SSL vocabulary pedagogically needs a teacher, not
an algorithm.

## 18. Where is machine learning in your component?

**Be precise here. Overclaiming is the trap.**

> "One place: **MediaPipe HandLandmarker**, a pre-trained convolutional model from
> Google that turns a camera frame into 21 hand landmarks. It runs in the
> browser, on the GPU where WebGL is available.
>
> Everything after that is **not** machine learning. DTW is a classical dynamic
> programming algorithm, and the learner model is an explainable heuristic. That
> was a deliberate choice, and I'd defend it: the component's job is to explain
> *what* you did wrong, and a classifier gives you a label and a confidence,
> neither of which can be turned into a corrective hint without inventing the
> explanation.
>
> The transformer work in our project title is my teammates' components —
> recognition, and the Whisper-based speech pipeline. Mine is deliberately the
> non-learned half, and it's what makes the learning module independent of
> whether the recognition model is finished."

## 19. Did you train or fine-tune a model?

**⚠ Weak spot. Do not dress it up.**

> "No. I did not train or fine-tune any neural network. I used Google's
> pre-trained HandLandmarker as-is.
>
> What I did instead was **fit the scorer's parameters to data rather than
> assume them**, and I'd argue that's the equivalent methodological work:
>
> - The score anchors were originally guessed at 0.05 and 0.35. Measuring 557
>   takes showed that was badly wrong — the zero point sat *below* the median
>   correct rendition, so a genuinely correct attempt scored zero. They're now
>   the measured p10 and p90.
> - The handshape/trajectory weighting was assumed at 0.7/0.3. A grid search over
>   11 weightings measured it, and it's now 0.8/0.2.
> - Two further changes were measured and reverted, including one that *improved*
>   the metric — because a unit test showed it let a learner moving in entirely
>   the wrong direction still score 100.
>
> Training a scorer is the natural next step, and it's blocked on data, not
> ambition: it needs learner attempts graded by an SSL teacher, which don't
> exist. The attempt log already records exactly what that model would train on."

## 20. Where is gamification in this component?

> "Four things, and one deliberate absence.
>
> **Practice sessions** — a finite set of five signs with a completion state, so
> something actually finishes. **Mastery bands** — New, Learning, Improving,
> Mastered. **A progress dashboard** with per-sign mastery, weakest first, and a
> daily activity chart. **A practice streak** in days. And **scenario role-play**
> — Restaurant and Introductions — which is goal-directed practice rather than
> drilling.
>
> The absence is the part I'd rather talk about. This is a research instrument
> used with human participants, so the motivation has to be honest — competence
> and progress, never manufactured pressure. So: **a session is always
> finishable** — a sign is done after one scored attempt whatever the score,
> because gating completion on reaching 85 would trap a beginner in an
> unfinishable loop. **Nothing is ever withheld** — the full vocabulary is
> reachable at all times. **Nothing is time-gated, and nothing is lost when the
> streak resets.** The streak is a progress readout, not a leash.
>
> Those constraints are written into the source as design bounds, not left to
> whoever builds the UI."

**⚠ If they push:** the deferred work is the other four gamified scenarios —
PP2 shipped Restaurant only, by plan. Full scope-cut rationale is in the PP2
Scope Plan.

## 21. What about authentication / login?

> "There is none, deliberately, and it's the right default for a research
> instrument. Everything lives in the browser's IndexedDB. No account means no
> credentials to leak, no personal data to hold, and a much simpler ethics
> position for the pilot — a participant's data physically cannot reach us
> unless they export it and hand it over.
>
> The cost is real and I'd name it: **progress is per-browser.** Clear your site
> data and it's gone; use another machine and you start fresh.
>
> When we add accounts — and Suvana will need them, since my teammate's component
> already has Auth.js and MongoDB — the shape is: **optional** sign-in for
> cross-device sync, local-first by default, and video still never uploaded.
> Unified sign-on across the four modules is explicitly post-viva work; it's an
> integration problem, not a research one."

## 22. "Private and on-device — no video ever leaves your device." Prove it.

**This is the strongest answer in the set. It is a structural proof, not a
promise. Offer to demonstrate it.**

Four pieces of evidence, in increasing strength:

1. **There is no backend.** The deployment is static files. There is no server
   of ours to receive video.
2. **The model runs locally.** MediaPipe's WASM runtime (11 MB) and the
   `hand_landmarker.task` model (7.8 MB) are served from **our own origin** —
   `public/wasm/` and `public/models/` — not a CDN. Inference happens in the
   page.
3. **The source contains exactly two network calls**, and both are same-origin
   GETs for static JSON:
   ```
   src/storage/bundledReferences.ts:56   fetch(INDEX_URL)
   src/storage/bundledReferences.ts:91   fetch(`${BASE_URL}references/${file}`)
   ```
   No POST. No `XMLHttpRequest`, no `sendBeacon`, no WebSocket, no analytics SDK.
   **There is no code path that could upload a frame.**
4. **The built bundle confirms it.** Every absolute URL literal shipped to the
   browser is a W3C XML namespace or a documentation link — `w3.org`,
   `gsap.com`, `react.dev/errors`. Not one API endpoint.

> "You can check it live — open the network tab, run a full practice attempt, and
> you'll see the reference JSON load and nothing else go out."

Frames are also **never persisted**: an attempt stores gloss, score, worst
fingers and a timestamp. Not landmarks, not video.

## 23. How is progress saved? Does it do telemetry? Does the model train on users?

**Three questions. The third is the one that matters — answer it hardest.**

> "Progress is in **IndexedDB**, in the learner's own browser. One database,
> `ssl-learn`, three stores: scored attempts, feedback-latency samples, and any
> reference recordings made locally.
>
> Mastery, strongest and weakest signs, the activity chart and the streak are all
> **derived from the attempt log on read** — nothing is stored twice, so they
> can't drift out of sync. The streak counts consecutive local days, and
> yesterday still counts as live, so a learner who hasn't opened the app yet
> today hasn't lost anything.
>
> **There is no telemetry. None.** No analytics, no crash reporting, no usage
> pings. I verified that against the built bundle — there's no endpoint in it.
>
> **And no, the model does not train on users. There is no learning loop at
> all.** The hand-tracking model is Google's, frozen, and identical for every
> user. The scoring constants are fixed at build time. Nothing a learner does
> changes the system for anyone — including themselves, beyond their own local
> mastery numbers. If we ever did train on learner data, that would need explicit
> consent, and it would be a research protocol, not a background process."

The one deliberate exception: the **pilot export**, which the participant
triggers themselves and hands over. It carries a participant *code*, never a
name.

## 24. What would make this an exceptional, marketable product?

Answer in three tiers so it reads as a roadmap, not a wish list.

**Tier 1 — what it needs to be credible research (Sep–Oct, planned):**
- **Our own signer-validated reference video.** Reference quality bounds
  everything the scorer can do, and a landmark skeleton can't show palm
  orientation. This is the single highest-ceiling change.
- **The pilot**, which converts two unmeasured targets into results.
- **Learner attempts graded by an SSL teacher** — closes the ≥90% target and
  creates a dataset nobody has.

**Tier 2 — what makes it a good product:**
- **Face and body tracking**, restoring non-manual markers to the score.
- **BKT + reinforcement-learning curriculum**, replacing the heuristic — and a
  pedagogically-ordered cold start instead of alphabetical (§17).
- **The other four scenarios**, and error mining across attempts to find the
  mistakes a learner repeats.
- **Optional accounts** for cross-device progress.
- **Sinhala localisation** of the interface.

**Tier 3 — what makes it marketable:**
- **Licence-clean data we own** — the precondition for everything commercial (§13).
- **Institutional packaging**: cohorts, an instructor view, assessment reports.
- **Deaf-community partnership as the business model**, not an afterthought.
- **Full Suvana integration**, so learning sits beside live translation — learn
  the sign, then use it, in one product.

> "If I had to pick one: **our own dataset, recorded with a Deaf teacher.** It
> unlocks the research target, the reference quality, the commercial licence and
> the credibility, all at once. Everything else is downstream of it."

## Two things a sharp panel might catch

**The hero's preview cards show "88% MATCH", "5 Day Streak", "Mastery: 84%".**
Those are illustrative mockups of the interface — the equivalent of a product
screenshot — not a learner's data. The functionality behind each is real. If
asked, say that plainly; don't let it look like a claim.

**`calibration/` is gitignored, so on a fresh clone the calibration tests
`describe.skip` and go silently green.** A passing suite alone doesn't prove the
calibration ran. If you offer a live reproduction, check the corpus count printed
in `calibration-report.md`. Details in [EVIDENCE.md](EVIDENCE.md) §5.
